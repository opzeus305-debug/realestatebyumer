Ground truth

Dubai does not need selling. It needs reading.

The market publishes everything. Most people never look. These are the figures I start every conversation with, dated and sourced, and I update them here when the Land Department publishes.

Three structural facts sit underneath the numbers: freehold ownership for foreigners in designated areas since 2002, a ten-year renewable Golden Visa at an AED 2,000,000 DLD-certified property value, and no tax on what you earn from an asset or what you sell it for. They are not a pitch. They are the terms.

The number the brochures leave out is the cost of getting in: 4% to the Land Department plus agency, trustee and mortgage fees — call it 7–8% on top of the price. I put it in the model before we talk about yield.

The analyst

An ex-banker in a market of salesmen.

I spent the first part of my career inside the exchange houses — UAE Exchange, then Al Rostamani — where a decimal point was the difference between a good day and a resignation. I moved into Dubai property because I kept watching intelligent people buy on enthusiasm and sell on panic.

My job now is to be the person in the room who has already read the file: the developer’s record, the payment schedule, the service charges, the rental comparables, the exit. Then to tell you, plainly, whether it holds.

The method

Four steps. No step skipped.

01

Read

Your position, not a product. Budget, currency, timeline; whether you want yield, growth, a residence visa, or all three. Twenty minutes, usually on WhatsApp.

02

Model

The shortlist as a ledger: entry price, payment schedule to handover, expected rent, service charges, projected exit. Every assumption written down where you can see it.

03

Select

One or two recommendations, with reasons. What could go wrong, and what we would do about it.

04

Hold & Exit

Handover, tenant, management, and the moment to sell or refinance. I stay on the file.

You receive the model as a one-page document — the same page I would want if I were the buyer.
